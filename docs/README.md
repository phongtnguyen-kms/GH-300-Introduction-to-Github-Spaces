# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

These documents define how OctoAcme plans, executes, tracks, releases, and improves cross-functional projects.

OctoAcme follows a lightweight but structured lifecycle that moves work from initiation through planning, execution, release, and retrospective learning. Projects start with a clear problem statement, success metrics, stakeholder alignment, early risks, and a go/no-go decision before teams invest in detailed planning. Approved work is then broken into prioritized, testable increments with milestones, dependencies, release expectations, and a documented definition of done.

Delivery is organized around clear cross-functional roles. Project Managers coordinate timelines, risks, status updates, and stakeholder communication, while Product Managers define outcomes, prioritize the backlog, and guide trade-off decisions. Developers implement and document solutions, contribute estimates, and surface technical risks, and QA or testing contributors validate acceptance criteria and critical flows. Stakeholders and sponsors stay involved through approvals, demos, and milestone reviews to keep scope and business priorities aligned.

Communication and escalation are treated as part of delivery, not overhead. Teams use regular standups, weekly delivery and PM/product syncs, milestone demos, and stakeholder updates, supported by a single source of truth such as a project board, README, or release document. Risks and blockers are tracked in a simple risk register and escalated from the team to the PM, Product Lead, and sponsor when business impact or cross-team coordination requires faster decisions.

Quality assurance is embedded throughout execution and release. OctoAcme expects small pull requests when practical, links to issues and acceptance criteria in PRs, CI-based testing and linting before review, and at least one approval before merge. Teams combine unit, integration, smoke, security, and manual QA practices as needed, then use retrospectives and incident follow-ups to convert lessons learned into tracked improvements.
